About me

Adebanji is a mathematical engineer and scientific machine learning practitioner whose research interests sit at the intersection of mathematical modelling, numerical methods, and machine learning. He is drawn to problems where a model has to respect an underlying physical or mathematical structure — inverse problems, computational imaging, and PDE-constrained systems — rather than treating learning as a black box.

He earned a B.Sc. in Mathematics from Obafemi Awolowo University, Nigeria (2018), followed by an M.Sc. in Mathematical Engineering from the University of L'Aquila, Italy (2021). His thesis, Lower Bounds on the Mix Norm of Passive Scalars Advected by Incompressible Enstrophy-Constrained Flows, combined variational analysis and functional inequalities with pseudo-spectral numerical simulation to study optimal mixing in incompressible flows.

Since then, he has built production machine learning systems in industry: aviation telemetry classifiers and FFT-based signal-processing pipelines at Intuos Srl, predictive failure models at N-and Italia, and geometric algorithms for FEA meshing at Bridgestone EMIA. In parallel, he has worked on inverse problems and computational imaging — image restoration, differentiable and inverse rendering, and medical image segmentation — and has developed end-to-end LLM systems, including a medical RAG assistant and LoRA/QLoRA fine-tuned models.
