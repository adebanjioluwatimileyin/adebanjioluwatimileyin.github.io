About me

Adebanji is an applied mathematician and machine learning researcher working at the intersection of mathematical modelling, scientific computing, and data-driven methods for scientific and engineering problems.

His training began with a B.Sc. in Mathematics (First Class Honours) from Obafemi Awolowo University, Nigeria, followed by an M.Sc. in Mathematical Engineering from the University of L'Aquila, Italy, with coursework spanning PDEs, functional analysis, numerical analysis, optimization, and dynamical systems. His Master's thesis, Lower Bounds on the Mix Norm of Passive Scalars Advected by Incompressible Enstrophy-Constrained Flows, derived analytical bounds on passive-scalar mixing in constrained incompressible flows and validated them against a pseudo-spectral numerical solver — an early instance of the pattern that now defines his work: mathematical analysis paired with computational experiment.

That foundation has carried into machine learning research. His current work extends phase-field methods for multiphase-flow PDEs toward learning-augmented solvers, alongside research in inverse problems, computational imaging, and physics-informed neural networks. In parallel, he has built and deployed machine learning systems in industry — aviation telemetry classifiers, FFT-based signal-processing pipelines, and predictive models for engineering systems — translating mathematical and computational ideas into working software.

He is seeking PhD and research positions in scientific machine learning, applied mathematics, and computational imaging.
