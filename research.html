Research

My research combines mathematical modelling, numerical methods, and machine learning to understand and solve computational problems arising in science and engineering. The themes below are organized around research questions rather than technologies; each links to the projects that provide evidence for it.

Scientific Machine Learning

Question: how can learning-based methods be combined with, rather than substituted for, the mathematical structure of a physical system? Foundation: PDE theory and numerical analysis provide the constraints — conservation laws, boundary conditions, known symmetries — that a learned model should respect. Method: physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) enforce the PDE residual, initial condition, and boundary condition directly through automatic differentiation, so the network is trained without labelled interior data. Current work is extending a phase-field solver for multiphase-flow PDEs — coupling an Allen–Cahn interface model, incompressible Navier–Stokes, and an energy equation with an FFT-based pressure solver — from a validated 2D/1D prototype toward 3D, as a foundation for future learning-augmented solvers in this setting. Relevant work: PINN for the 1D advection-diffusion equation, phase-field modelling of multiphase-flow PDEs.

Applied Mathematics & PDEs

Question: what are the fundamental limits on mixing, transport, and diffusion in a constrained PDE system, and can they be derived analytically rather than only observed numerically? Foundation: functional analysis, variational methods, and functional inequalities. Method: deriving analytical bounds (e.g. on the H⁻¹ mix norm of a passive scalar under an enstrophy-constrained velocity field), then validating them computationally with pseudo-spectral and finite-element solvers. This was the subject of my Master's thesis, and remains the mathematical core of how I approach new problems — state the bound, then build the solver that tests it. Relevant work: M.Sc. thesis on optimal mixing of passive scalars, phase-field PDE modelling.

Inverse Problems & Optimization

Question: given corrupted, partial, or indirect observations, how do you recover the underlying signal, image, or physical parameters? Foundation: variational formulations, convex and non-convex optimization, and — for differentiable rendering — gradient-based optimization through a physical forward model. Method: total-variation regularization solved via Douglas–Rachford splitting for image restoration; a pure-PyTorch differentiable physically-based renderer (Cook-Torrance BRDF) that recovers spatially-varying material maps from multi-view images by backpropagating through the rendering equation itself. Relevant work: differentiable PBR material recovery, TV-regularized image inpainting.

Computer Vision & Medical Imaging

Question: how do you extract reliable structure — an organ boundary, a 3D scene, a clean image — from noisy or high-dimensional visual data? Foundation: convolutional and volumetric deep learning architectures, combined with domain-specific priors (foreground-biased sampling for class-imbalanced medical volumes; tensor decomposition for efficient 3D scene representation). Method: a 3D U-Net for liver segmentation from abdominal CT, trained with a combined Dice/BCE loss and sliding-window inference; from-scratch implementations of NeRF and TensoRF for neural scene reconstruction, compared quantitatively under matched conditions. Relevant work: 3D liver segmentation from abdominal CT, NeRF vs. TensoRF neural radiance fields.

Signal Processing & Engineering Systems

Question: how do you extract a physically meaningful signal from real-world sensor data, and turn it into a reliable operational pipeline? Foundation: Fourier analysis and spectral estimation. Method: FFT-based frequency-peak detection for engine RPM estimation from acoustic signal alone, and telemetry-based classifiers for real-time flight-phase identification, deployed as production pipelines rather than offline analyses. Relevant work: aircraft engine RPM estimation via audio DSP, flight data monitoring at Intuos Srl.

Future Directions

I am particularly interested in extending this work toward probabilistic and uncertainty-aware formulations — quantifying confidence in learned surrogates for PDE-constrained systems, and data-efficient learning where labelled data is expensive or physically constrained, as is typical in scientific and engineering settings.
