Research

Numerical PDEs & Scientific Computing

How do you know a PDE solver is actually correct, rather than merely plausible-looking?

Foundation

Spectral and finite-element discretisation theory, a priori error estimates, and the method of manufactured/exact solutions as the standard for trusting a new solver.

Approach

Every solver in this portfolio is checked against an exact or manufactured solution and a measured convergence rate before it is used for anything else: fourth-order temporal and spectral spatial convergence for a pseudo-spectral Navier–Stokes solver, O(h³)/O(h²) convergence for a Taylor–Hood finite-element solver, O(h²)/O(h¹) convergence for a plane-stress elasticity solver.

Current research

This verify-before-trust discipline is the common thread across an otherwise varied portfolio: the same standard applied to a spectral CFD solver, a finite-element elasticity solver, and a pseudo-spectral mixing solver from my Master's thesis.

Selected work

  • → 2D Navier–Stokes: Pseudo-Spectral Solver + POD-Galerkin ROM
  • → 2D Elasticity FEM + SIMP Topology Optimisation
  • → M.Sc. Thesis: Optimal Mixing of Passive Scalars

Computational Fluid Dynamics

How do incompressible and multiphase flows behave once you commit to reproducing a published benchmark exactly, rather than a qualitatively reasonable flow?

Foundation

Incompressible Navier–Stokes in both vorticity-streamfunction and velocity-pressure form; conservative Allen–Cahn phase-field interface tracking for multiphase flow.

Approach

A Fourier pseudo-spectral solver for periodic-domain flow; a Taylor–Hood FEniCSx/PETSc solver for bounded-domain flow past an obstacle; a phase-field solver coupling Allen–Cahn interface dynamics, Navier–Stokes, and an energy equation for vapour-liquid phase change.

Current research

Quantifying the convergence of the DFG Schäfer–Turek cylinder-wake benchmark (steady and unsteady) with curved geometry and audited force definitions, and developing the phase-field boiling solver toward 3D: a dedicated 1D Stefan solver converges at first order against the analytical benchmark and the 3D implementation is numerically verified, not yet physically validated, exploiting a constant-coefficient FFT pressure-Poisson solve that carries over unchanged.

Selected work

  • → FEM Cylinder Flow: Schäfer–Turek Benchmarks
  • → Phase-Field Multiphase-Flow Modelling
  • → 2D Navier–Stokes Pseudo-Spectral Solver

Inverse Problems & Optimisation

Given corrupted, partial, or indirect observations, how do you recover the underlying signal, image, or physical parameter – and how do you know the recovery method itself is not the bottleneck?

Foundation

Adjoint-based PDE-constrained optimisation, Tikhonov and total-variation regularisation, and variational/gradient-based methods more broadly.

Approach

Adjoint gradients (verified by Taylor-remainder tests) for recovering a spatially varying PDE parameter; self-adjoint sensitivity analysis for SIMP topology optimisation; total-variation regularisation via Douglas–Rachford splitting for image restoration; a differentiable renderer and a synthetic acoustic forward model paired with learned refinement, for material and photoacoustic-image recovery respectively.

Current research

The Darcy permeability inversion is the clearest example of a diagnosed method limitation rather than a hidden one: an H¹-seminorm regulariser reconstructs a smooth truth well but cannot recover sharp, localised structure at any regularisation strength – a regularisation-model mismatch, reported rather than smoothed over.

Selected work

  • → Deterministic and Bayesian Darcy Permeability Inversion
  • → SIMP Topology Optimisation
  • → Sparse-View Photoacoustic Tomography
  • → Differentiable PBR Material Recovery
  • → TV-Regularised Image Inpainting

Reduced-Order & Scientific Machine Learning

Can a fast, learned or reduced approximation of an expensive PDE solve be trusted outside the exact setting it was built from?

Foundation

Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD), Galerkin projection, the Discrete Empirical Interpolation Method (DEIM) for hyper-reduction, neural operators, and physics-informed neural networks (PINNs).

Approach

A tensorial POD-Galerkin ROM with DEIM hyper-reduction for 2D Navier–Stokes, with reduced operators verified against the projected full-order equations to machine precision; a four-way comparison of POD-Galerkin, DEIM, an MLP surrogate, and a Fourier Neural Operator for viscous Burgers' equation; a PINN that learns a linear PDE's solution directly from its residual, with no labelled interior data.

Current research

A recurring, deliberately preserved negative finding: a snapshot-POD basis built from a handful of training realisations reconstructs its own training data almost perfectly, but a genuinely new, unseen realisation at matched physical parameters can be captured almost not at all. This is reported as a real limitation of the method for flows without a fixed spatial structure, not engineered away.

Selected work

  • → POD-Galerkin ROM + DEIM for 2D Navier–Stokes
  • → ROM, Hyper-Reduction & Neural Operators for Burgers' Equation
  • → PINN: 1D Advection-Diffusion

Dynamical Systems & Data Assimilation

How do you estimate the state of a chaotic system from sparse, noisy observations, and when does the estimator itself fail?

Foundation

Lyapunov-exponent analysis of chaotic sensitivity, and ensemble-based (Kalman-filter) sequential state estimation.

Approach

A stochastic Ensemble Kalman Filter with perturbed observations and Gaspari–Cohn covariance localisation for the chaotic Lorenz-96 system, independently validated against a linear/Gaussian Kalman filter with a known closed-form solution.

Current research

Characterising filter failure as directly as filter success: severe ensemble under-dispersion without localisation, and very small ensembles that can settle into a stable but persistently wrong state, are documented alongside the headline >11× RMSE improvement localisation provides.

Selected work

  • → Ensemble Kalman Filtering for Chaotic Lorenz-96
  • → M.Sc. Thesis: Passive-Scalar Mixing in Incompressible Flow

Uncertainty Quantification

Once a point estimate is recovered, how much can its uncertainty actually be trusted, and how do you tell?

Foundation

Bayesian inverse problems, Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC), and ensemble-based uncertainty estimation.

Approach

A Karhunen–Loève-parameterised Gaussian prior sampled by preconditioned Crank–Nicolson (pCN) MCMC for Bayesian Darcy-permeability inversion, with the sampler itself independently validated against an analytically tractable linear-Gaussian posterior before being trusted on the full PDE problem.

Current research

Diagnosing when a posterior or ensemble estimate is NOT trustworthy is treated as seriously as reporting when it is: effective sample size and autocorrelation are checked per Karhunen–Loève dimension rather than summarised by a single acceptance rate, and MAP rather than the posterior mean is reported as the primary estimate wherever that check fails.

Selected work

  • → Bayesian Darcy Permeability Inversion
  • → Ensemble Kalman Filtering (Lorenz-96)

Computer Vision & Medical Imaging

How do you extract reliable structure, an organ boundary, a 3D scene, a clean image, from noisy or high-dimensional visual data?

Foundation

Convolutional and volumetric deep learning architectures, combined with domain-specific priors: foreground-biased sampling for class-imbalanced medical volumes, tensor decomposition for efficient 3D scene representation.

Approach

A 3D U-Net for liver segmentation from abdominal CT, trained with a combined Dice/BCE loss and sliding-window inference; from-scratch implementations of NeRF and TensoRF for neural scene reconstruction, compared quantitatively under matched conditions.

Current research

The two most recent projects sit at opposite ends of the same problem: extracting structure from a fixed volumetric grid (CT) versus from unstructured, view-based signals (NeRF/TensoRF).

Selected work

  • → 3D Liver Segmentation from Abdominal CT
  • → NeRF vs TensoRF Neural Radiance Fields

Signal Processing & Engineering Systems

How do you extract a physically meaningful signal from real-world sensor data, and turn it into a reliable operational pipeline?

Foundation

Fourier analysis and spectral estimation.

Approach

FFT-based frequency-peak detection for engine RPM estimation from acoustic signal alone, and telemetry-based classifiers for real-time flight-phase identification.

Current research

Both pipelines process real signals rather than offline analyses alone: an FFT-based engine monitor tested against real Tecnam P92/Rotax 912 recordings (a personal prototype, not flight-certified), and a flight-phase classifier running in production at Intuos Srl.

Selected work

  • → Aircraft Engine RPM Estimation via Audio DSP
  • → Flight Data Monitoring at Intuos Srl

Future Directions

I am interested in combining PDE-based mathematical models, numerical simulation, and machine learning to develop surrogate, reduced-order, and inverse models for physical systems, including fluid dynamics and data assimilation. A recurring theme in my work is understanding where data-driven approximations agree with the underlying physics, where they fail, and how those discrepancies can be quantified: a snapshot-POD model that fails on unseen flow realisations, or a Bayesian posterior whose effective sample size is too low to trust, are as informative as a model that succeeds. I am particularly interested in extending this work toward probabilistic and uncertainty-aware methods, including Bayesian and ensemble-based uncertainty quantification for learned PDE surrogates and data-efficient learning in settings where labelled data are expensive, sparse, or physically constrained.
